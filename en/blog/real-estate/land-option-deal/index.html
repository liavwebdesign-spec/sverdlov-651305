An Option Agreement to Purchase Land: How It Works, and Why to Tread Carefully

What makes a land option binding, the unique option conditions for tax, the 24-month limit and the 30-day notice, and what happens when they are missed.

An option agreement gives one side, the option holder, the right but not the obligation to purchase a particular plot of land within a defined period, at a price fixed in advance or under an agreed price-setting mechanism. It is a common tool in real estate development, because it lets a developer lock in a plot for a period of examination or fundraising without committing to an immediate purchase.

What Makes an Option Binding

An undertaking to carry out a transaction in land requires a written document under section 8 of the Land Law, and an option agreement is subject to that requirement as well. Beyond the writing requirement, a properly drafted option agreement expressly addresses the exercise period, the price or the mechanism for setting it, and whether the option holder enjoys exclusivity during the term of the agreement.

An Option and a Memorandum of Understanding Are Not the Same Thing

There is a tendency to confuse an option agreement with a memorandum of understanding (zichron dvarim). A memorandum of understanding is usually an interim document summarizing intentions ahead of a full transaction, and its legal status depends on the drafting and the circumstances. Courts sometimes recognize it as a binding contract and sometimes do not. A well-drafted option agreement, by contrast, is intended from the outset to be a binding document that is clear about each side's rights and obligations.

The Tax Angle: What a "Unique Option" Is

Section 49I of the Land Taxation Law recognizes a special category of unique option, which is not treated as a taxable event when granted. To fall within that category the option must meet all of the following conditions: it was granted in writing; the seller's undertaking to sell is irrevocable; the option is transferable and exercisable on its terms; it is exercisable within 24 months from the day it was first granted by the holder of the right in the land, so that extensions and further options are counted within that same period; the consideration paid for the option itself does not exceed 5% of the value of the right in the land or of the exercise price, whichever is higher; no right of possession in the land was given to the option holder, and this is an absolute condition with no materiality threshold; and notice of the grant of the option, or of its sale, was given to the Director of Land Taxation within 30 days. That last condition is the one most transactions fail on, and it cannot be extended.

What Happens When the Option Is Not a Unique Option

An agreement that does not meet the conditions can still be valid and binding as a matter of contract, but the tax picture is entirely different. The Israel Tax Authority may treat it as a sale of a right in land already at the time of the grant, meaning capital gains tax (mas shevach) for the seller and purchase tax (mas rechisha) for the option holder immediately, plus a further purchase tax event on every onward assignment of the option. This is precisely the outcome the unique option structure is designed to avoid, and it is why the conditions should be examined before signing rather than after.
