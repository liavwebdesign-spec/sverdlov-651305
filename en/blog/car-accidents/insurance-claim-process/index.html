What the Claim Process Against the Insurance Company Looks Like

The stages of a claim against the insurer: notice and medical records, prompt payment within 60 days, disability assessment, settlement or court, limitation.

Many people injured in a road accident do not know what to expect from the process itself. Broadly speaking, it involves several main stages.

Notifying the Insurance Company and Medical Documentation

The first stage is to notify the relevant insurance company of the accident, and to gather and document all the medical material relating to the injury: emergency room records, treatment summaries, imaging and x-rays, and referrals for further investigation.

Prompt Payment: A Right Many People Do Not Know About

You do not have to wait until the end of the process to receive money. Section 5 of the Law requires the insurer to make a prompt payment (tashlum takuf) to the victim for medical expenses and urgent needs, within 60 days of receiving a written demand supported by documents. This is one of the most practical and useful rights available to a victim at the early stage, precisely during the period when expenses are mounting and income has stopped. That said, it is granted for exceptional cases and urgent needs.

Determining the Degree of Disability

Disability is determined by an expert appointed by the court, or by a medical committee of the National Insurance Institute (Bituach Leumi).

Settlement or Court Proceedings

A large share of claims end in a settlement with the insurance company, without full court proceedings. Where no agreement is reached, or where the company rejects the claim, the next option is to file a claim in court.

The Limitation Period

As a rule, the limitation period for a claim under the Road Accident Victims Compensation Law is seven years from the date of the accident. There is an important exception for minors: the limitation clock only starts running on their 18th birthday, so in practice they have until the age of 25. Bear in mind that other deadlines arising from the same event are far shorter, for example 12 months for an injury allowance claim to the National Insurance Institute, and five years under a private insurance policy.

When to Approach a Lawyer

There is no need to wait for an advanced stage of the process before seeking advice. Getting in touch early, close to the accident, is precisely what makes it possible to guide the collection of medical documentation from the outset, keep to the short deadlines, and avoid mistakes that are hard to put right later, such as giving statements to the insurance company without guidance.
