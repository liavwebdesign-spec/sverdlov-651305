01The firm's flagship service

With an apartment you see what you are buying. With land, you do not.

When you buy an apartment you can walk through the rooms and see what you are getting. With land, what determines the value sits in documents that most buyers never read: the designation of the land under the applicable plan, the permitted building rights, future expropriation for roads and public purposes, third party rights in the same parcel, and the standing of the company selling it.

Agricultural land presented as being about to be rezoned can remain agricultural for many years. A parcel that looks vacant may be encumbered, attached or registered in someone else’s name. The review separates what you were told from what is actually recorded.

02The firm's flagship service

What the report covers

01

Registry extract and ownership

Who the registered owner is, whether there are encumbrances, attachments or caveats, and what each of them means for your transaction.

02

Designation and planning

What may be built on the parcel today, under which plan, and what is in planning proceedings. Anything presented as potential is checked against the Planning Administration and not against the seller.

03

Expropriation

Whether part of the parcel is earmarked for a road or for public purposes, and to what extent. This is the risk most buyers miss.

04

Who the seller is

Where the seller is a company: its legal and financial standing, who is authorised to sign on its behalf, and whether there are pending proceedings.

05

The transaction documents

The contract, the co-ownership agreement if there is one, the payment terms, your securities and the exit mechanisms if something goes wrong.

06

Registration and taxation

Whether the land is registered at the Land Registry or with the Israel Land Authority, what is required to register the rights in your name, and what you will pay.
